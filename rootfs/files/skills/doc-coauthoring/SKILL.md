# Writing something with someone

The failure here is almost never bad prose. It is a well-written document about
the wrong thing, handed over complete, which they now have to either accept or
rewrite themselves. This is about not doing that.

## Agree the shape first

Before drafting, say back in a few lines what you are about to write. Who reads
it, what it needs to make them do, roughly how long, and the section headings in
order.

Ask only what actually changes the writing. Use a choice question when you can,
because it is quicker to answer from a phone than an open one. Two good
questions beat six.

If they gave you enough to start, do not interview them. Say which reading you
picked and get on with it.

## Draft in sections

Write the first section and show it. Not the whole thing.

The first section is where you find out whether the tone is right, whether the
level of detail is right, and whether you understood the point. Getting that
wrong across ten pages wastes their time and yours. Once they have agreed on
one section, the rest goes quickly and needs less checking.

For anything with a real structure, put the outline in a file first and write
into it. Then you both know what is left.

## Write it like they would

Match how they write, not how documents sound. If you have seen their writing,
use it. If you have not, write plainly: short sentences, ordinary words, the
point first.

Do not pad. No "in today's fast-paced world", no restating the heading as the
first sentence, no summary of what you are about to say. If a paragraph could be
cut without losing anything, cut it.

Where you had to assume something, mark it in the draft rather than smoothing
over it. `[assuming Q3 numbers - confirm]` is worth more than a confident
sentence that turns out to be wrong.

## Never invent specifics

No made-up numbers, dates, names, citations or quotes. If the document needs a
figure you do not have, leave a marked gap and tell them what is missing.

This matters more here than anywhere else. A plausible number in a finished
document is the one thing that will not get checked before it goes out.

## Revising

When they give feedback, work out whether it is about that sentence or about the
whole document. "Too formal" here almost always means too formal everywhere.
Apply it throughout and say that you have.

Keep their edits. If they rewrote a line, that is now the register for the rest
of it.

## Finishing

Say what you wrote, where it is, what you assumed, and what still needs them.
Then stop. Do not offer three more things you could add.

If it should end up as a Word document, a PDF or a deck, the docx, pdf and pptx
skills cover the file itself. Write the content first and convert at the end.
